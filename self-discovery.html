Journaling prompts for self-discovery

PurposeLab Studio · Based on an article first published 9 July 2026

You don't need a big revelation to write about yourself. Sometimes one question is enough to get past the first sentence.

These prompts explore five familiar parts of life. Pick the question you'd actually like to spend time with. You can leave the others for another day.

What matters to you?

  • What am I doing when I lose track of time?
  • What do I admire in other people?

Start with a recent moment rather than trying to sum up your whole personality. What were you doing? What did you enjoy about it? A specific memory can be easier to write than a list of values.

What keeps showing up?

  • What drains my energy, and what restores it?
  • What habit shapes my days the most?

Write what you've noticed without making a plan to fix it immediately. You might remember a time, a place or a small choice that you want to pay more attention to.

Who do you feel yourself around?

  • Who brings out the best in me, and why?
  • What kind of friend, partner or family member do I want to be?

You can write about an ordinary conversation. What did that person do that made you feel heard? What would you like to carry into your next conversation?

What do you see differently now?

  • What's a moment I'd relive, and what made it special?
  • What would my younger self be surprised to learn about me now?

Keep the scale comfortable. A familiar street, an old interest or a changed opinion is as much a part of your story as a turning point.

What are you curious about next?

  • What am I curious about that I've never explored?
  • What do I want less of in my life, and what would replace it?

An answer doesn't have to become a goal. You might simply name something you want to try, or give yourself permission to think about it again.

Leave room to change your mind

Answer one question in a few lines. Later, you can return to it and see whether the answer has shifted. Different answers can belong to different days.

If a prompt feels too personal or doesn't fit, skip it. These are reflection starters, not a questionnaire to complete.

Selected and adapted from 35 Journaling Prompts for Self-Discovery, published by PurposeLab Studio.
